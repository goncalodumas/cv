# Gonçalo Dumas Diniz

*Curious by nature. Practical by preference. Occasionally organised.*

## Hello!

I'm a Portuguese professional with a rather unconventional career path, a broad range of interests, and a persistent curiosity about how things work.

My professional journey has taken me through computer-aided design, geographical information systems, architecture, web development, IT support, and infrastructure management.

It hasn't always been a straight line. In fact, it rarely has.

What connects these experiences is a preference for practical problem-solving, understanding complex systems, organising information, and learning by doing.

I enjoy taking things apart — sometimes literally — understanding how they work, and finding ways to make them better.

## What I do

### IT, infrastructure, and technical support

I currently work in IT support and infrastructure management at **Sermail**, while also providing on-site IT support at **Daiichi Sankyo Portugal**.

My work includes troubleshooting, equipment management, technical support, infrastructure maintenance, and improving the way information and resources are organised.

I particularly enjoy the challenge of turning poorly documented or disorganised systems into something understandable, manageable, and maintainable.

### Geographical information systems and technical design

Before moving into my current roles, I spent over two decades at **Nemus**, working with GIS, CAD, cartography, spatial analysis, and technical information management.

My work supported environmental studies, engineering, and planning projects in Portugal and internationally.

Along the way, I also became involved in graphic design, technical illustration, web development, and building online platforms for presenting information and supporting public participation.

### Web development and information systems

I've been interested in web technologies for many years, particularly content management systems and tools that make it possible to build applications around real-world needs.

Drupal was a long-standing companion. More recently, I've been exploring Payload CMS, PostgreSQL, and modern development workflows, increasingly with the assistance of AI.

I'm not a software engineer by training. I approach development from the perspective of someone who understands a problem and wants to build a useful solution.

## Things I'm working on

### Asset and maintenance management

Designing and developing an internal asset and maintenance management system, bringing together technical equipment records, maintenance planning, work orders, and intervention histories.

The project combines operational experience with data modelling, application design, and AI-assisted development.

### Retrocomputing

I collect, preserve, and occasionally repair vintage computers, particularly machines from the 1980s and 1990s.

It's a hobby that combines my interests in electronics, computing history, restoration, and understanding how technology evolved.

I'm also developing a digital catalogue of my collection, using Payload CMS.

### Experiments and side projects

I enjoy exploring new technologies, building small tools, and experimenting with ideas that may or may not become finished projects.

Some work out. Others become learning experiences. A few remain permanently under construction.

## Learning by doing

I've always preferred hands-on learning.

Give me a real problem, the tools to investigate it, and enough room to experiment, and I'll probably learn more than I would from hours of abstract explanations.

Technology keeps changing, and keeping up isn't always easy. But the curiosity is still there.

I'm particularly interested in the ways new tools, including AI, can help people with practical experience turn ideas into working solutions, without necessarily having to master every underlying technology first.

## A little more about me

I'm curious, sometimes stubborn, often too ambitious about what can be achieved in the available time, and occasionally guilty of focusing on details at the expense of the bigger picture.

I appreciate thoughtful design, clear documentation, reliable tools, and work that has a tangible purpose.

I take my work seriously. Myself, considerably less so.

My approach to organisation could probably be described as a recurring cycle of Big Bang, Big Crunch, and occasional moments of remarkable order.

*Rinse and repeat.*
